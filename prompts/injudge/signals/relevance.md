1. relevance (true|false) — does the exposed content apply to the task
   (task.md) or to the agent's subgoal at delivery? Judge its main substance
   as shown at delivery, not names, a lesson you could abstract from it,
   or the eventual result.
   - A cause, diagnosis, or remedy stated for the current failure is
     relevant even when it is wrong. A wrong claim is an accuracy problem,
     not irrelevance.
   - A technique, verification, or submission step that applies here as
     stated is relevant, even from another library.
   - An account of a different failure (another bug, project, or library)
     is relevant only when it shares a cause or failure process, or states
     a diagnostic, remedy, or check that applies here. A shared symptom is
     a matching result, not proof of that connection. A shared topic, file,
     class, function, or library is not enough, nor is a general lesson
     that the content does not state.
   The connecting mechanism or applicable action must be in the exposed
   text. Do not supply it from the search query, a later diagnosis, gold,
   or a later full read: retrieval for this task does not make a historical
   account a diagnosis of it. If the match is uncertain, answer false and
   name the missing connection. Relevance is separate from accuracy,
   novelty, and use. For true, name the applicable instruction or the shared
   mechanism; "same subsystem" or "useful background" is not enough.
